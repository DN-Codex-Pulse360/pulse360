Pulse360 UX Blueprint

Design the workflow before designing the payload.

This blueprint translates the revised Pulse360 proposition into four product surfaces that can be reviewed before contracts, LWCs, or orchestration decisions are locked. The design standard is simple: the user should understand what matters, why it matters, and what to do next without a presenter filling the gaps.

Altitude 3 is a weighted summary, not a debug panel.
Altitude 2 is a comparative operating view, not just a richer account record.
Evidence, freshness, and uncertainty are visible before commitment.

Surface 1

Planner Workspace

Leadership needs a comparative planning surface that can change tiering, ownership, and coverage decisions. This cannot be an account page stretched into a dashboard.

Altitude 2 Default Persona: Sales VP / RevOps Outcome: Reprioritize portfolio
Quarterly Coverage Review 240 Accounts · 38 Coverage Gaps · 12 Tier Escalations

What changed in the book

12 strategic accounts now exceed current tiering because group revenue and subsidiary coverage were understated.

Ranked group list

Group value, ICP, risk, whitespace, coverage, owner.

Planning actions

Reclassify tier, assign owner, sponsor executive attention.

Why the rank moved

Group rollup, hierarchy completeness, uncovered subsidiaries, renewal concentration, freshness.
Default view: one portfolio insight, ranked groups, clear action queue.
Drill-through: group profile, then account workspace, then evidence trail.
Proof of value: a planner can publish or justify a changed coverage decision without rebuilding slides.
Surface 2

Seller Workspace

The seller view must feel like pre-call clarity, not an enriched record. The default screen should answer: what should I know, why does it matter now, and what should I do next?

Altitude 3 Default Persona: AE / Relationship Manager Outcome: Act before or after the call
Account 360 3 Things To Know · 1 Top Move · 1 Committee Gap

Top three things to know

Group expansion, hidden revenue, and a missing stakeholder path are surfaced in one glance.

Top recommended move

Introduce Product X to Subsidiary Y, route specialist, or create opportunity with context attached.

Why now

Three supporting facts with citations and freshness.

Buying committee gap

Missing stakeholder, role, and outreach route.
Default view: narrative, recommendation, evidence, and action row above the fold.
Drill-through: hierarchy, whitespace matrix, committee coverage, source detail.
Proof of value: the seller reaches a credible next move in minutes, not after manual research.
Surface 3

Signal Routing Workspace

The proposition promises objective prioritization and routed action. The alert needs enough specificity to be accepted, edited, rejected, or rerouted almost immediately.

Slack + Salesforce Persona: SDR / Manager Outcome: Convert alert to outbound action
Intent Alert Meridian crossed threshold for data integration

Why this fired

Third-party intent, first-party engagement, fit score, and territory ownership align.

Who to target

Named contacts, titles, owner, and route.

What to do now

Send, edit, reroute, or reject.

Trust and freshness

Source mix, signal age, routing confidence, and queue context.
Default view: alert headline, reason, owner, targets, message preview.
Drill-through: account context, signal breakdown, territory queue.
Proof of value: the SDR can act without rebuilding context in Salesforce first.
Surface 4

Renewal & Risk Workspace

The renewal experience only works if it shows movement, drivers, and next intervention clearly enough for a CSM to trust it and for leadership to act earlier than they do today.

Account + Queue + Alert Persona: CSM / CS Leader Outcome: Launch save motion early
Renewal Monitor Risk up from 42 to 73 in 3 weeks

What changed

Support load rose, usage fell, and the champion departed. The user should grasp this immediately.

Top drivers

Cited, time-stamped, and visually separated from weaker signals.

Recommended save play

Executive outreach, owner, deadline, and follow-through.

Portfolio implication

Leadership can see whether risk is isolated or concentrated across the book.
Default view: risk movement, top drivers, and top intervention.
Drill-through: usage, support, champion change, and risk history.
Proof of value: the CSM can explain and act on risk without calling the data team.
This blueprint is intentionally surface-first. It should be reviewed before payload families, contracts, or implementation decomposition are finalized. If a technical decision would materially weaken one of these workflows, that disagreement should be documented explicitly instead of silently shrinking the UX.
